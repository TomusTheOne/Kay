Three directions for
kaydiving.com

Three complete homepage concepts, built from scratch as static pages. They are deliberately as different from each other as I could make them — not three coats of paint on one layout. Each one carries the same content, the same booking engine and the same SEO scaffolding, so the only variable you are judging is the design.

A

Xibalba

Cinematic and mythic. The page is a dive: scrolling is descending, a depth gauge tracks you from 0 to 40 metres, and the water darkens as you go. Sells wonder.

kaydiving.com — concept A
Open the concept to view it →
  • The mechanic: a fixed depth gauge that counts metres as you scroll, with zone labels — Surface, Cavern zone, Deep, The abyss.
  • Type: Instrument Serif for display, Inter for text, IBM Plex Mono for instrument data.
  • One warm colour. Everything is cold turquoise except the booking action, which is torchlight amber. Impossible to miss.
  • Booking: a diver's underwater slate that fills in as you choose, stamped “Not confirmed”.
  • Best if Kay wants to sell the emotion of the Mayan underworld and stand apart from every other Tulum dive shop.

B

Luz

A printed travel journal. Paper, hairlines, air, and photographs treated as plates. Almost no motion — the speed is the design. Sells taste and trust.

kaydiving.com — concept B
Open the concept to view it →
  • The mechanic: asymmetric editorial layout, numbered essays, a drop cap, and an honest typographic index of every cenote.
  • Type: Newsreader for display, Karla for text. Warm, calm, expensive.
  • Fastest of the three. One IntersectionObserver and a fade. No parallax, no scroll maths, no canvas.
  • Built-in SEO engine: the cenote index table and a visible Journal section give Google real content to rank.
  • Best if Kay wants to read as a boutique, personal operation — closer to a good hotel than a dive shop.

C

Bitácora

The dive logbook. Limestone, ink and one acid signal colour on a visible grid. Every site states its depth, visibility, level and price. Sells competence.

kaydiving.com — concept C
Open the concept to view it →
  • The mechanic: a filterable dive-site index (level, type) and a depth-profile chart plotting every site against the 21 m cavern limit.
  • Type: Space Grotesk for display, JetBrains Mono for every number on the page.
  • A live status bar across the top — water temperature, visibility, departure times, guide ratio.
  • Strongest for SEO: structured, factual, answer-shaped content is exactly what ranks for “cenote diving Tulum depth” queries.
  • Best if Kay wants serious, repeat divers who choose a shop on standards rather than on photographs.

Read this before you judge the copy

What is real and what is placeholder

  • Placeholder: all prices, opening hours, the phone number and the postal address. They are plausible for Tulum but they are not Kay's — they need confirming before anything goes live.
  • Placeholder: any claim about certification agency, team size or years in business. I deliberately avoided inventing an owner's name or biography.
  • Real: the cenotes, their depths, the certification levels, the 21 m cavern limit, water temperatures, the Sac Actun figures and the reef facts. Worth a sanity check with Kay, but researched rather than invented.
  • Illustrations, not photographs. Every image is hand-drawn SVG so the maquette has no licensing problem and loads instantly. In production these become Kay's own photographs — the layouts are built to take them.
  • Copy is in English because that is the booking language for Tulum dive tourism. Every concept is wired for EN / ES / FR with hreflang already in place.
