The place

Rain falls on the Yucatán and disappears. There are no rivers here — the limestone swallows everything and carries it underground, through one of the largest flooded cave systems on earth, out to the sea. Where the rock has collapsed, you can see in. The Maya called those openings dzonot. We call them cenotes.

They were sacred long before they were beautiful. Offerings went into them. So, at times, did people. In the Popol Vuh they are the way into Xibalba, the place below — and standing at the edge of one at seven in the morning, with the mist still on the water, it is not hard to see why.

Kay Diving exists to take you a short, respectful distance inside. Always on a guideline. Always within sight of daylight. Never more than four of you at once, because a cavern with eight divers in it is a cavern nobody can see.

The reef

Salt water, twenty minutes out. The Mesoamerican Barrier Reef is the second largest on earth and it runs right past Tulum. Turtles on the seagrass most mornings, eagle rays over the sand, and bull sharks up the coast from November.

How we work

  • Four divers per guide, maximum. In a cavern that is a safety decision before it is a comfort one.
  • Courses run with two students. You leave when the skills are yours, not when the schedule says so.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen only. We keep spare tubes in the van for the people who forget.
  • We will talk you out of a dive. If conditions or your logbook say no, we say no and move the booking.

Questions

Before
you come.

Do I need to be a certified diver?

For cavern diving, yes — Open Water is the minimum, and Angelita or The Pit need Advanced. If you are not certified we run guided cenote snorkelling and Discover Scuba in the open, daylight sections, which are beautiful in their own right.

What is the difference between cavern and cave diving?

Cavern diving stays in the natural daylight zone — within about 60 metres of the entrance, no deeper than 21 metres, always on a permanent guideline with a direct route up. Cave diving goes past daylight and needs its own certification. Everything we guide is cavern.

How cold is the water?

Around 24–25 °C in the cenotes every day of the year, 26–29 °C on the reef. A 3 mm wetsuit suits most people; we carry 5 mm at no extra cost if you run cold.

What is a halocline?

Fresh rainwater floating on heavier salt water. The boundary refracts light, so swimming through it looks like the water has turned to oil — then it closes behind you and the view snaps back.

When should I come?

Cenotes are sheltered, so they dive well all year. For the light beams aim for 11:00–13:00 between March and September. Reef conditions are best March to June.

Can you pick me up from my hotel?

Yes — included from Tulum town, $25 from the hotel zone, $45 from Akumal or Playa del Carmen. Tell us where you are staying when you book.
