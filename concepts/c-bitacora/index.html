04 — How we run it

The rules we
will not bend.

R—01

Four divers, one guide

In an overhead environment this is a safety number, not a comfort number. We do not run six.

R—02

Daylight zone only

Always on a permanent guideline, always within sight of the exit, never past 21 metres in a cavern.

R—03

We check your logbook

Not to be difficult. Dreamgate punishes poor buoyancy and the formations do not grow back.

R—04

We will cancel

If conditions, rainfall or your recent experience say no, we move the booking. You are not charged.

R—05

Reef-safe only

No oxybenzone anywhere near the water. We keep spare tubes in the van for the people who forget.

R—06

Nothing is touched

No gloves in the caverns, no souvenirs, no exceptions. The system is 376 km long and entirely finite.

06 — Questions

FAQ

What certification do I need?

Open Water is the minimum for every cavern dive on this page — Casa Cenote, Dos Ojos, Dreamgate, Calavera and the reef. Angelita and The Pit go past 18 metres, so they require Advanced Open Water. We check logbooks and we do not make exceptions.

Cavern or cave — what am I actually allowed to do?

Cavern diving stays inside the natural daylight zone: within roughly 60 metres of the entrance, no deeper than 21 metres, always on a permanent guideline with a direct route to the surface. Cave diving goes beyond daylight and needs a dedicated cave certification. Everything we guide is cavern.

How cold is it and what suit do I need?

Cenotes sit at 24–25 °C every day of the year, the reef at 26–29 °C. A 3 mm wetsuit suits most divers. We carry 5 mm at no extra charge if you run cold — say so when you book.

What is a halocline?

The boundary where fresh rainwater floats on heavier salt water. The two refract light differently, so swimming through it looks like the water has turned to oil or frosted glass — then it closes behind you and the view snaps back.

When is the best time to come?

Cenotes are sheltered from wind and swell, so they dive well all year. For the light beams at The Pit and Dos Ojos, book the 11:00 departure between March and September. Reef conditions are best March to June.

Do you pick up from hotels?

Included from Tulum town, $25 from the hotel zone, $45 from Akumal or Playa del Carmen. Tell us where you are staying when you book.
